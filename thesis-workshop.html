how the day runs

A room. Three windows. One sentence by 6pm.

9 — 12

Diagnostic

Two exercises. A three-minute "why am I here" round, then sticky-notes on three colors: what's working, what's broken, what you've been carrying alone. The third one does most of the work.

12 — 4

Thesis

One sentence — who, in what moment, replacing what. We test it against the team's three different versions, against the deck, and against the customer you talked to last week. Always takes longer than people expect.

4 — 6

The handoff

The conversation that turns the sentence into the team's next move. We don't ship you a plan; we hold the time so the room can produce one — what changes Monday, what to test in 30 days, what to leave alone for now.

how the room actually moves

Five moves. Every workshop.

the sentence frame

Every articulation gets tested against three questions: who, in what moment, replacing what.

"We're not a quick-commerce app. That's a channel. We're the place she goes when she's standing in front of her closet and feels like she's already wearing it wrong."

A real workshop output, fuzzed for confidentiality — from a room with Klydo. Replaces a category claim ("quick-commerce app") with a moment ("standing in front of her closet").

the check-in

Three minutes per person. Why are you here, in your own words.

"to keep this from becoming the wrong company" "to find out if we still agree" "because I don't trust the deck anymore"

Forces specificity before any frame is in the room. Adjacent answers are normal; identical answers are rare. The differences map the work.

the sticky-notes diagnostic

Three colors. Three questions. The third one does most of the work.

Green — what's working that we should protect. Red — what's broken in how we work. Yellow — what you've been carrying alone.

Silent for five minutes, then we read the wall together. The yellow notes are the ones the room hasn't been able to talk about.

the pressure-test

Run the candidate sentence against three things — and rewrite if it fails any of them.

vs. the deck — does the sentence survive a slide? 01
vs. the team's three current versions 02
vs. the actual customer you talked to last week 03

A sentence that survives all three is rare on the first pass. Most rooms rewrite it twice between 2pm and 4pm.

the write-up

Nothing leaves the room verbal.

So the work doesn't quietly get retconned by Wednesday.

a quick read

Six questions. Two minutes. Real read.

A quick read on whether we're the room. We'll route you to the right next step — or tell you we're not it.
