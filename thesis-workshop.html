From the workshop
how the day runs

A room. Three windows. One sentence by 6pm.

9 — 12

Diagnostic

Two exercises. A three-minute "why am I here" round, then sticky-notes — green / red / alone. The silent stuff comes out.

12 — 4

Thesis

One sentence — who, in what moment, replacing what. Always takes longer than people expect.

4 — 6

90-day plan

2–3 archetypes, operating principles in writing, a 14-day experiment cell with named owners.

what you walk away with

Five things in writing.

the sentence

One line the leadership team can say back to each other.

"We're not a quick-commerce app. That's a channel. We're the place she goes when she's standing in front of her closet and feels like she's already wearing it wrong."

A real workshop output, fuzzed for the client. Found in the room around 4pm. Replaces a category claim ("quick-commerce app") with a moment ("standing in front of her closet").

the archetypes

Two or three customers you commit to first.

Priya · 27 · Bangalore Marcus · 41 · Brooklyn Sana · 34 · Karachi

Named, with real demographic and behavioral specificity. What replaces "25–35, urban metro, high intent."

the principles

How the team runs experiments — in writing.

Small bets first. Fast read on signal. No long builds without a fast version. Define success per surface up front.

Signed off by the room. Most have been violated quietly in the prior quarter.

the plan

A 90-day plan with named owners.

W1–2 Ship closet-moment landing test PM
W3–4 3 creator partnerships, same hook GM
W5–8 Re-cut onboarding around archetype 1 DES
W9–12 Read win conditions; cut or commit CEO

Three to five experiments. Pre-committed win conditions. Named owners — initials on the page so accountability survives Wednesday.

the summary

A written record of what was decided.

So the work doesn't quietly get retconned by Wednesday.

a quick self-test

How to know you're ready.

  1. You've tried to write the company in one sentence twice in the last quarter and the team didn't agree on either version.
  2. Investors say "interesting" but don't move. The deck has been "almost" for two months.
  3. You've noticed yourself answering the same internal question — what are we, exactly — three different ways in three different rooms.

If two land, book the diagnostic. If all three, skip it and book the workshop.
