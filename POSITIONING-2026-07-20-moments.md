## The spine (settled)

> **Joyus is two people who both hold the whole — the decisive move *and* the made thing — each sharper at one end, so nothing falls through the crack between strategy and craft, because for us there is no crack. We come in for the things that decide the trajectory, tight and intense, then leave. We price by what the moment is worth to you, not by the hours or the deliverable — which is why a fundraise is not a branding project. And when the moment is big enough, we would rather own a piece of the outcome than send an invoice.**

### Why the pairing is the product

- Kahran is sharper at leverage (the decisive move — the raise, the launch, the pivot). Divya is sharper at craft (the made thing). **But both are genuinely good at both.** This is overlap, not division of labor.
- That overlap means **no seam.** A handoff duo — strategist hands to designer — leaks value at the join: the strategist doesn't really get craft, the designer doesn't really get leverage. Joyus has no join. Each can push back on the other's edge and *earn* the pushback, because each has done the other's job and knows the other is better at it.
- The respect between them is grounded in shared competence, not diplomacy. Neither is *only* their strength, so neither can be reduced to the support role. This is why it works, and why it has to be equally Divya's.

### What we're actually selling

- **Not deliverables.** "Branding," "a strategy," "a deck" are the *forms* the work takes. Sell those and you're a vendor priced against other vendors.
- We sell **leverage on decisive work, delivered by a two-person unit that holds the whole thing.** The unit of sale is *a decisive thing, handled end to end, by two people who can both see the move and make it real.*

### How we work — the container (settled 2026-07-20)

There is **one shape of work and one relationship state.** That's the whole commercial model.

- **The Intensive is the only shape of work.** An immersive burst — days, sometimes up to ~3 weeks — that both *decides the move and makes the thing.* The craft comes out the other end of the same burst; it is not deferred to a long production tail. Go in hard, deliver the whole decisive thing, leave. Intensity is the unit, **not duration** — and this immersive making is the work Kahran and Divya both love, not a compromise the model tolerates.
- **There is no "Cycle."** No long, humming, retainer-shaped engagement runs in the background. Even the big things are done as an intensive (or a few of them, stacked).
- **The Stake is ownership, not labor.** When we believe in something we take a piece — and *that piece* is what keeps us around. "Staying in touch" is *ownership*, not being on call: we're owners watching it grow, not a vendor doing ongoing tasks. This is what preserves the intermittent life-shape — the relationship rides on the stake, the *work* stays spiky.
- **The relationship is a portfolio, not a client list.** One company might buy four intensives over four years — the raise, the launch, the next raise, the sale — and between them we just hold a piece and stay warm. We don't graduate a client *up in scope*; we convert them *from paid to owned.*

**The whole model on a napkin:** one way we work (come in hard, make the thing, leave) × one variable (do we get paid, or do we take a piece).

### How we price

Price tracks **how much the moment is worth to you**, not the craft or the hours. One ruler, cash at one end, a share of the upside at the other:

| The work | What sets the price | Payment tilts toward |
|---|---|---|
| Lower-stakes / craft-defined (e.g. an identity) | Scope & craft | **Cash** |
| High-stakes / trajectory-defining (a raise, a launch, the pivot) | What the outcome is worth | **Cash + a share of the upside** |

- Equity is not a bolted-on option. It's the **far end of the same ruler** — the natural unit of payment for the biggest moments, the way cash is the natural unit for the small ones.
- The **form** of the upside fits the buyer (see below): startup → equity; a product or a book → revenue share / royalty; a raise or a sale → a success fee; a cash-rich established business → just high cash.
- Client can prefer the upside deal (cash-poor, upside-rich) **or** we can prefer it (we believe in you and want the ride). The engagement is fixed and short; the *stake* is what varies.

### The freedom underneath (keep OFF the site as a claim)

- The whole model exists so work can be **intermittent** — high value per engagement, no always-on obligation, so a month off is not a big deal.
- Never state this on the site. Let it show as **selectivity**: "we take a few engagements a year." Selectivity signals the whole life-shape without explaining it, and is itself a premium signal.

## Who it's for (settled — this is the part Kahran loved)

**The buyer is defined by their *situation*, not their *category*.** Not "startup founders." Tying it to founders would (1) shrink us into the most crowded, most commoditized niche there is, (2) chain the upside model to cap tables, and (3) miss the best buyers.

The buyer is **anyone at a genuine inflection who has enough at stake — and enough resource — that a premium two-person engagement pays for itself, and who wants the move *and* the made thing from one pair they trust** instead of stitching a consultant to an agency. That cast:

- **A founder** raising / launching / pivoting. (Upside = equity, native here.)
- **A person launching themselves** — an author, a creator, a public figure, someone repositioning a career or platform. The move + the made thing applies to a *person's* launch as much as a company's.
- **An established business at an inflection** — a rebrand tied to a real strategic shift, a spinoff, a next-generation handoff of a family business, a sale that needs to be told as a story. Pays cash, big.
- **A mission or movement at its decisive moment** — a nonprofit, a campaign.

**Say who it's NOT for.** We take a few engagements a year, for people at a real inflection with real stakes. We are not a vendor for routine work. Naming who we turn away is what makes the selectivity legible.

## How we present it (OPEN — unresolved)

Two things are decided:
- **Do NOT organize by service** ("Branding / Strategy / Fundraising") — that's a menu; makes us a vendor.
- **Do NOT organize by audience** ("For Founders / For Brands") — forces a category, shrinks us.

One thing tried and **rejected:**
- **Organize by "moment"** ("You're raising · You're launching · You're becoming something new"). Rejected — it flattens rich, specific humans into transactional event-labels and points the camera at the client's calendar instead of at the people. It was the services menu wearing a disguise.

The **cast of people is the keeper.** The open question is what the site is *organized around* instead. Live candidates:

1. **Portrait-forward — the people ARE the structure.** A gallery of humans told as stories (*the founder who's outgrown the story they started with · the maker becoming a public figure · the business that's quietly become bigger than its category*). The buyer sees *themselves in a person* and feels met. Warm, uncategorizable.
2. **The two of you, and who you go in with.** Lead with the pairing (you + Divya, the no-seam thing); the cast appears as "the kinds of people we bet on" — evidence of *you*. Closest to the original "about meeting Divya and me."
3. **The bet itself.** Organizing unit = you place bets on people; the site is the gallery of bets. Foregrounds conviction/equity. Bold, or mercenary, depending on tone.
