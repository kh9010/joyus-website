Who it's for, and what they use instead

six personas · four wants each

The ask was a brand. The work turned into a question first: who is this club for, and what are they doing on a Tuesday night without it?

WhoWhat they wantToday they useWhat the club gives

The Quiet Connector

$400K HHI, spends on what compounds. Two kids, 9 and 12. Soccer sideline, porch coffee, house project.

A way to be away from his usual social circles.a coffeeshop near the house, or the officeWe give himThe café, the lounge, the events space. Wellness and the bays. Featured chefs, poker nights, golf-focused pilates — and leagues that give you somewhere to go even when you don't have friends.
Running into someone he hasn't seen in a while.kids' sports, community events
A space that reminds him of freer times.the driving range, a lakefront patio
Wellness on Monday morning, before work.at home, a small private gym

The Country-Club Guy

$600K+, asset-rich. Teens at home. 7:40 tee time, home by noon, lake with the kids. Already belongs somewhere.

A simulator that's free when he wants it.his houseWe give himBookings he can plan around — an 8am bay before work. Demo days, whiskey launches, Bandon and Pebble. Deliberately slower programming: visits over two hours.
Dropping in without the long commute.the local gym
Thursday night poker.a friend's house, a private room at Daniel's
Wellness — most clubs don't offer it.a scheduled appointment with his wife

The Semi-Retired Operator

$2M+ net worth, lives off investments and board fees. Grown kids, first grandchild. Two boards, one advisory role.

Meeting younger, more interesting people.the country club, concerts, dinnersWe give himA named role and a real position in the club. He organizes programming, recruits teams, hosts member dinners — and we say publicly that he is the one who made it happen.
Being a practical mentor to someone.board work, informal coffees
Being valued and noticed for it.charity events, winery concerts
Throwing something impromptu on a Friday.the country club

The New-to-Seattle Founder

$250K plus equity. Newly married, no kids, a dog. New restaurant, one hike, Sunday work.

A true second space.a coffeeshopWe give himThe lounge as workspace and as the default "meet me at." A room mixed on purpose — professions, genders, backgrounds. Events with the top Seattle companies, one-offs, short-format matches.
Somewhere to take video calls.a coffeeshop
Somewhere to meet investors.a coffeeshop
People who aren't colleagues.meetups, affinity groups, friends of friends

The Empty-Nester

$500K HHI, house paid off, college fund done. Kids in their 20s. Long breakfast, garden, dinner with two other couples.

Reconnecting with the hobbies they dropped.the arts centre, the mall, a purchaseWe give themCohorts that start the game on the same day, so nobody is behind. Leadership called out by name, and accolades worth winning. Private space to book — book clubs, birthdays, corporate parties.
Friends who match the life they have now.friends of friends
Seeing people without hosting at home.a favourite restaurant
Being respected for what they know.charity events

The Mid-Career High Earner

$450K single or $700K dual, no dependents. Senior IC or top firm. Run, brunch, date night, side project.

A third place.a barWe give herGuest passes, and the generosity of bringing people. Athlete and brand nights, demo sessions, date nights worth choosing. Faster programming — in and out in sixty to ninety minutes.
Plans that are certain when she needs them to be.the neighbourhood bar
People to watch the game with.a sports bar, the work text group
Knowing the next thing before it's the next thing.work, Instagram

The mix

The founding cohort is cast like a school class: a mix of industries, ages, kinds. A block of 8–20 from one consulting firm asked to join together and was turned down.

Then the brand

Four visual directions, a brand guide, a sales playbook, and eastwestclub.com, all built on the grid. After our part, the club brought in someone to run events and carry the brand into the space. It opened, and its first 25 members are in.
