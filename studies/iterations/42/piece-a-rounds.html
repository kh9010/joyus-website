04

What they use instead

27 May 2026 · workshop, FigJam

For each persona: what they want, where they get it today, and what the club gives them instead. The substitutes are the competition, and almost none of them are clubs.

The Quiet Connector

$400K HHI, spends on what compounds. Two kids, 9 and 12. Soccer sideline, porch coffee, house project.

  • A way to be away from his usual social circles.Today: a coffeeshop near the house, or the office
  • Running into someone he hasn't seen in a while.Today: kids' sports, community events
  • A space that reminds him of freer times.Today: the driving range, a lakefront patio
  • Wellness on Monday morning, before work.Today: at home, a small private gym
We give himThe café, the lounge, the events space. Wellness and the bays. Featured chefs, poker nights, golf-focused pilates — and leagues that give you somewhere to go even when you don't have friends.

The Country-Club Guy

$600K+, asset-rich. Teens at home. 7:40 tee time, home by noon, lake with the kids. Already belongs somewhere.

  • A simulator that's free when he wants it.Today: his house
  • Dropping in without the long commute.Today: the local gym
  • Thursday night poker.Today: a friend's house, a private room at Daniel's
  • Wellness — most clubs don't offer it.Today: a scheduled appointment with his wife
We give himBookings he can plan around — an 8am bay before work. Demo days, whiskey launches, Bandon and Pebble. Deliberately slower programming: visits over two hours.

The Semi-Retired Operator

$2M+ net worth, lives off investments and board fees. Grown kids, first grandchild. Two boards, one advisory role.

  • Meeting younger, more interesting people.Today: the country club, concerts, dinners
  • Being a practical mentor to someone.Today: board work, informal coffees
  • Being valued and noticed for it.Today: charity events, winery concerts
  • Throwing something impromptu on a Friday.Today: the country club
We give himA named role and a real position in the club. He organizes programming, recruits teams, hosts member dinners — and we say publicly that he is the one who made it happen.

The New-to-Seattle Founder

$250K plus equity. Newly married, no kids, a dog. New restaurant, one hike, Sunday work.

  • A true second space.Today: a coffeeshop
  • Somewhere to take video calls.Today: a coffeeshop
  • Somewhere to meet investors.Today: a coffeeshop
  • People who aren't colleagues.Today: meetups, affinity groups, friends of friends
We give himThe lounge as workspace and as the default "meet me at." A room mixed on purpose — professions, genders, backgrounds. Events with the top Seattle companies, one-offs, short-format matches.

The Empty-Nester

$500K HHI, house paid off, college fund done. Kids in their 20s. Long breakfast, garden, dinner with two other couples.

  • Reconnecting with the hobbies they dropped.Today: the arts centre, the mall, a purchase
  • Friends who match the life they have now.Today: friends of friends
  • Seeing people without hosting at home.Today: a favourite restaurant
  • Being respected for what they know.Today: charity events
We give themCohorts that start the game on the same day, so nobody is behind. Leadership called out by name, and accolades worth winning. Private space to book — book clubs, birthdays, corporate parties.

The Mid-Career High Earner

$450K single or $700K dual, no dependents. Senior IC or top firm. Run, brunch, date night, side project.

  • A third place.Today: a bar
  • Plans that are certain when she needs them to be.Today: the neighbourhood bar
  • People to watch the game with.Today: a sports bar, the work text group
  • Knowing the next thing before it's the next thing.Today: work, Instagram
We give herGuest passes, and the generosity of bringing people. Athlete and brand nights, demo sessions, date nights worth choosing. Faster programming — in and out in sixty to ninety minutes.

Word for word from the EWC brand guide, "Audience · what we replace" (personas.json).
