Client: East West Club, Seattle

You have the value. You can't break it down yet.

East West Club is a new members club with golf bays, a café and a lounge. It had a lot to offer, and said it the same way to everyone.

So the two of us at Joyus broke it into six kinds of member, with what each one wants. The club put them in its brand guide.

The six we made
  1. The Quiet Connector
  2. The Country-Club Guy
  3. The Semi-Retired Operator
  4. The New-to-Seattle Founder
  5. The Empty-Nester
  6. The Mid-Career High Earner
88%of founding members signed. Faster than they expected.

What each one wants, and where they go for it now

The competition wasn't another club. It was what they'd do otherwise.

The Quiet Connector

A way to be away from his usual social circles. Today: a coffeeshop near the house, or the office

The Country-Club Guy

A simulator that's free when he wants it. Today: his house

The Semi-Retired Operator

Meeting younger, more interesting people. Today: the country club, concerts, dinners

The New-to-Seattle Founder

Somewhere to meet investors. Today: a coffeeshop

The Empty-Nester

Seeing people without hosting at home. Today: a favourite restaurant

The Mid-Career High Earner

People to watch the game with. Today: a sports bar, the work text group

their brand guide, one of four wants for each

Where it came from

We ran two workshops with the founders. They wrote on the board; we mostly listened.

“If East West Club didn't exist, what would be missing from your life?”

  • I would not have a friend circle that is specifically from the EWC.
  • If i am a golfer, i would not have the convenience. And a coach.
the founders, workshop board

Sound familiar?

For the club, being stuck meant this: until members signed, there was no money to build. Until it was built, there were no next members.

It's the thing that keeps coming back to you. Everything else is waiting on it.

A piece like this costs $5–10K, with the two of us.
