# Study 87 brief: EWC, the value broken down, said plainly

_Written 7 Nov 2026 from the blind judges' run on Study 86 (`tasks/w883en23o.output`). Replaces the slab/prism line of 85-86._

## Why a new brief

Study 86 built four versions. Our own paper judge scored them 35-37 of 42 on the bet. Blind readers who only saw phone screenshots gave them **18.5-22.5 of 42**. Every reader gave **0 of 2** on "Do I feel clear after ten seconds, or tired?" Three of the eight ten-second readers would not keep reading.

The ideas held up. The surface didn't. Readers liked the six member types, the competition line and the 88%. They also said "I can't tell what they sell or what the deliverable was." The page made them decode it before it gave anything back. So this brief starts from what the readers took in, not from the concept.

## Who reads it, and where

Divya's bet (`studies/sitemap/bet.json`): **a founder or creative, sent by someone they trust, missing one piece.** Two readers in practice, both on a phone and both busy:

- the founder of a small members club or hospitality business, sent the link by a friend who said "these two are good";
- a creative director at a small brand, quietly stuck on something, scrolling between meetings.

They have never heard of Joyus or EWC. They give the page ten seconds. They aren't going to tap anything to find out what it means.

## The first phone screen (390 x 844), in plain words

When the first screen has gone by, the reader must be able to say, without guessing:

1. **Whose page this is.** Joyus, a two-person studio. The header wordmark is the plain text **"Joyus"**: no dashed box and no logo slot.
2. **Who the client is.** East West Club, written out in full before "EWC" ever appears, plus what it is: a new members club (golf bays, a café, a lounge; these come from the guide's own give lines).
3. **Their problem, as the reader's problem.** Lead with c's line, which readers kept as the best hook in the set: **"You have the value. You can't break it down yet."** Then say straight away who had it: the club had a lot to offer and said it the same way to everyone.
4. **What the studio did.** In one sentence that names a thing, e.g. *we broke it into six kinds of member: what each one wants, and what they'd do instead*. Readers complained more than anything else that the page "never says plainly what they deliver".
5. **Ideally, that it worked.** The 88% can be on screen one.

The blind judge's own recipe: "c's headline on d's first screen". That means the headline, then at once one plain sentence on why the client came, then real evidence. Nothing abstract may sit between the headline and that sentence.

### What readers hit, which this page must not have

- **"They" with no one behind it.** Every pronoun needs a named subject on the same screen. "Two people" read as "team or client?". Write it as "two of us at Joyus".
- **EWC not spelled out** until halfway down, and confused with a golf club. Spell it out first.
- **Riddles.** "How would you split this?" at the top, "came for:", "turned out" as a bare label, "everything on offer, pooled". A question to the reader goes at the end, after the answer, never at the top.
- **Cryptic labels and code-like lines.** "the value, broken down · two people · [time] · $5–10K" read as code, and "$5–10K with no context (price? budget?)". Put facts in a sentence or give them plain labels ("what it cost: $5–10K").
- **Faint text.** The grey lists beside the triangle were "too pale to read". All text needs at least 4.5:1 contrast, body text at least 16px, and no label smaller than 13px.
- **Duplicate quotes.** The same sticky line showed up in the slab and again on a sticky note, which read as a glitch. Each client line appears once.
- **Visible placeholders and internal notes.** "wordmark", "[time, to fill]", "our draft, for Kahran to confirm", empty grey slabs, a blank input with no prompt, "the pace is drawn, not measured" (a disclaimer that cancels the 88%). **None of these, anywhere.**
- **§ marks**, mono section numbers and academic wayfinding. Drop them all.
- **Competing type styles.** Mono, serif, italics, sticky notes and § made the page "busy" and "tiring". Use one family and keep the two voices only (see below), separated by one quiet cue.
- **Things that need a tap to make sense.** "tap a beam", "touch the small tile", unlabelled coloured tiles and bars. Every visual reads static, with its own label. Interaction may add depth but never carries meaning.
- **Abstract diagrams that cost before they pay**: grey slab, prism, triangle, unlabelled week grids. If a picture needs a caption to explain the picture, cut it.
- **Layout breaks.** "happen/s" wrapping, "StrategyFor" with no space, the next-row flush to the screen edge, 88% split across screens. Give every block a margin, and keep every number with its words.

## Content that earned full marks, and must survive

Readers named these as the strongest part of every version:

- **The six member types** (`86/personas.json`, from the brand guide's "Audience · what we replace"), each with a one-line want and where they go for it today, verbatim: The Quiet Connector, The Country-Club Guy, The Semi-Retired Operator, The New-to-Seattle Founder, The Empty-Nester, The Mid-Career High Earner. **Never the profile line** (it carries income and family details). Wants, today-places and give lines only.
- **"The competition wasn't another club. It was what they'd do otherwise."** (ours) A reader called it "the one place the page shows real thinking".
- **"88% of founding members signed. Faster than they expected."** (ours, from the call on 6 Nov) A plain number in large type. No drawn pace chart and no disclaimer.
- **"You don't need a brief. It starts small."** (ours, the bet's line) The rank judge was sorry to see it cut from d. It closes the page.
- **"You have the value. You can't break it down yet."** (c's line, ours) It opens the page.

Optional, sourced, once each at most: the workshop question **"If East West Club didn't exist, what would be missing from your life?"** with up to three founders' sticky answers (one reader said the stickies were "the first thing that made sense"); the guide's **"Somewhere you never need an occasion to come in, only a reason to stay."**

## The bet, run as a page

Divya's model (`sitemap/bet.json`, Studies 69 and 72). **The sum:** nearly everything we've made says *look at us*; this page must say **"that's you, and you'd come out of this looking good."** The reader should see their own problem, and see the client come out looking smart. The studio is a helper.

Her three moves, in her order:

1. **Name the situation first.** Open on what was stuck, not on what we did. That is c's line, and then the club.
2. **Make the client the one who finds it.** Came for this, it turned out to be that, and they did this with it. Say it as plain sentences, not as three labels.
   - *Came for:* "to make it tangible where the club sits in a member's life".
   - *Turned out:* one club, six different reasons to walk in, and the competition was what each person does otherwise.
   - *Did with it:* the six went into their brand guide (that is where `personas.json` comes from), and 88% of founding members signed, faster than expected.
3. **Replace fear with the haunting.** On the ask, with a small first step: *"It's the thing that keeps coming back to you. Everything else is waiting on it."*, then "You don't need a brief. It starts small.", then one way in.

Readers gave 0 to the questions on what staying stuck costs (q6), whether I'm someone who acts (q8) and whether writing is safe (q19). Answer them in plain words:

- **Cost**, from Kahran's answer: until members signed there was no money to build, and until it was built there were no next members. Write it as one sentence, ours. Never as unlabelled tiles.
- **Safe**: one low-pressure way in. The email is hello@joyus.studio. A field is fine only if it has a visible label and a button. "Download the sheet" puzzled a stranger, so avoid it unless the page has already shown the sheet.
- A question back to the reader ("How would you have split yours?") is welcome, but **once, at the end**, after the answer.

## Scope

- **One** EWC case study, about **one part** of the work: **the value broken down**. Site, brand and sales kit appear at most as a quiet "more from EWC".
- **No timeline**, no month names, no process diary.
- **The Tuesday is at most one small piece**: a line or a single small visual inside the competition idea. Never the title, never a section of its own. The weekday readings (Mon steam, Wed slump, Thu out) are Kahran's reading and must be marked as ours if used.
- **Two voices**, from Kahran's answers. Our words are one voice (italic or another single cue). The club's verbatim words are the other (upright), each with a short plain source ("the founders, workshop board" / "their brand guide"). No third style.

## Length

**At most 5 phone screens**: 390 x 844, nothing tapped, header to footer included, so no more than about 4,200 CSS px of page height. Fewer is better. Our sentences: no more than 130 words on the page.

## Public-repo rules (unchanged and enforced by `check.cjs`)

- No client people's names and no prospect or workshop attendee names, anywhere in the page, `copy.json` or the index.
- No money but **$5–10K**, and no figure but **88%**. No income on member cards.
- Never "an easy weekday place".
- Client quotes verbatim from the sources (the workshop board by element id, `ewc/src/brand-guide.html`), with the board's spelling. Our reading is marked as ours.
- The club's home page is shown only as a picture (`86/img/front.png`), never transcribed.
- No em dash in our words. noindex.

## No visible placeholders, at all

- **Time**: omit it. No slot, no "[to fill]". The fact line is "two of us · $5–10K" or a sentence.
- **Client pride quote**: omit it. No empty quote frame, no hidden-but-spaced slot.
- **Wordmark**: the plain text "Joyus".
- No "our draft", no "for Kahran", no "to come" anywhere a visitor can see, the HTML source included.

## How it gets judged

Run the same blind screenshot judges as Study 86 (the ten-second test on screen one, the 21 bet questions on the whole page, then a rank). The bar: **every ten-second reader can name Joyus, East West Club, the problem, what the studio made and the 88%**, and nobody scores "tired". Our own paper scores don't count.
