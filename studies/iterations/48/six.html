Who East West Club is for, and what it is up against.

The Quiet Connector

$400K HHI, spends on what compounds. Two kids, 9 and 12. Soccer sideline, porch coffee, house project.

A calm senior title, weekends that belong to the family, an email that is just their name.

A third place that asks nothing.

Whether a club is a sound spend or an indulgence.

A way to be away from his usual social circles. Today: a coffeeshop near the house, or the office.

picking up kids and coming home, so they are busy, figuring out dinner

The café, the lounge, the events space. Wellness and the bays. Featured chefs, poker nights, golf-focused pilates — and leagues that give you somewhere to go even when you don't have friends.

The Country-Club Guy

$600K+, asset-rich. Teens at home. 7:40 tee time, home by noon, lake with the kids. Already belongs somewhere.

He will mention his club in the first five minutes. Senior exec, or his name is on the company.

Not a replacement — the weekday club.

Why a second membership.

A simulator that's free when he wants it. Today: his house.

Coaching baseball/doing something with atheletics

  • the local gym
  • a friend's house, a private room at Daniel's
  • a scheduled appointment with his wife

Bookings he can plan around — an 8am bay before work. Demo days, whiskey launches, Bandon and Pebble. Deliberately slower programming: visits over two hours.

The Semi-Retired Operator

$2M+ net worth, lives off investments and board fees. Grown kids, first grandchild. Two boards, one advisory role.

Sold it, stepped back, or halfway there. Talks about what they are building next — usually for other people.

A role, not a membership.

Whether this is something to do or merely somewhere to be.

Meeting younger, more interesting people. Today: the country club, concerts, dinners.

named roles: league captains, dinner hosts

  • the country club, concerts, dinners
  • board work, informal coffees
  • charity events, winery concerts
  • the country club

A named role and a real position in the club. He organizes programming, recruits teams, hosts member dinners — and we say publicly that he is the one who made it happen.

The New-to-Seattle Founder

$250K plus equity. Newly married, no kids, a dog. New restaurant, one hike, Sunday work.

Founder, operator, or investor. New to the city, or just ready for a new room — rooted founders run this play too. The first question, asked or not, is who else is a member.

The first circle.

Whether the people they want to know will actually be here.

A true second space. Today: a coffeeshop.

dinner with colleagues / clients

The lounge as workspace and as the default "meet me at." A room mixed on purpose — professions, genders, backgrounds. Events with the top Seattle companies, one-offs, short-format matches.

The Empty-Nester

$500K HHI, house paid off, college fund done. Kids in their 20s. Long breakfast, garden, dinner with two other couples.

Senior and easing off. Says some version of "I finally have time."

The next chapter, with structure.

Being new at something in public.

Reconnecting with the hobbies they dropped. Today: the arts centre, the mall, a purchase.

going to a game, to a museum to have dinner with friends

Cohorts that start the game on the same day, so nobody is behind. Leadership called out by name, and accolades worth winning. Private space to book — book clubs, birthdays, corporate parties.

The Mid-Career High Earner

$450K single or $700K dual, no dependents. Senior IC or top firm. Run, brunch, date night, side project.

Books things same-day. The visit will run sixty to ninety minutes. Often arrives with someone worth impressing.

Early is the privilege.
