Joyusa case study by a two-person studio

East West Club, Seattle

A new members club with golf bays, a café and a lounge.

the problem
The founders had a big, vague value: a new kind of social club, not a country club. It meant a lot to them, and nothing in particular to anyone else.
what we did
Worked out who the club is for and what it means to those people, as one problem. Then wrote it down as six member cards in the club's brand guide.
what it did
88%of founding members signed, faster than the club expected. That money is what let building start.

1 · the goal and the gap

The club had to feel accessible and desirable to the right people. We started by asking the founders.

"If East West Club didn't exist, what would be missing from your life?"

  • I would not have a friend circle that is specifically from the EWC.
  • going to a game, to a museum to have dinner with friends
  • maybe EWC can serve as a way of breaking monotony
the founders' answers, written on our workshop board

You have the value. You can't break it down yet.

2 · the hard part

Two things moved at once: who the club is for, and what it gives them. Fix one and the other shifts.

Aim at everyone and the club means nothing in particular. Aim too narrow and too few people sign. We looked for the point that holds. The competition wasn't another club. It was what they'd do otherwise.

4 · why it mattered

The club was stuck in a loop. The right members, signed first, broke it.
