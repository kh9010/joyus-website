East West Club, Seattle

We worked out who a new members club is for, and what it gives them.

The clientEast West Club, a new members club in Seattle with golf bays, a café and a lounge.
The problemA big idea, “not a country club, a new kind of social club”, that meant nothing in particular to anyone outside.
What we didFound the six kinds of people it could matter to, and what it gives each. Solved together, since each answer moved the other.
The result88% of the founding membership signed, faster than the club expected.

Where it started

You have the value. You can't break it down yet.

We asked the founders: “If East West Club didn't exist, what would be missing from your life?”

I would not have a friend circle that is specifically from the EWC.

maybe EWC can serve as a way of breaking monotony

dinner with colleagues / clients

a founder, on the workshop board

Every answer was true, and each pointed at a different person. The competition wasn't another club. It was what they'd do otherwise.

What we made

Six member pages for the club's brand guide. Here are two.

Member 4 of 6

The New-to-Seattle Founder

WantsGoes today to
A true second space.a coffeeshop
People who aren't colleagues.meetups, affinity groups, friends of friends
We give himThe lounge as workspace and as the default “meet me at.” A room mixed on purpose — professions, genders, backgrounds.

Member 3 of 6

The Semi-Retired Operator

WantsGoes today to
Meeting younger, more interesting people.the country club, concerts, dinners
Being a practical mentor to someone.board work, informal coffees
We give himA named role and a real position in the club. He organizes programming, recruits teams, hosts member dinners — and we say publicly that he is the one who made it happen.

The other four, by one want each:

Quiet ConnectorA space that reminds him of freer times.
Country-Club GuyA simulator that's free when he wants it.
Empty-NesterSeeing people without hosting at home.
Mid-Career High EarnerPeople to watch the game with.
