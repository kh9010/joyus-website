Joyuscase study: strategy

East West Club · Seattle

We worked out who East West Club is for, and what it means to them.

East West Club is a new members club in Seattle, with golf bays, a café and a lounge. Its value was big and vague: not a country club, a new kind of social club. That meant a lot to its founders, and nothing in particular yet to anyone else.

88%

of founding members signed, faster than the club expected.

Why the two had to be solved together

Each time we pinned down who the club is for, what it had to give them shifted. Each time we changed what it gives, a different person walked in. So we solved both at once, and looked for the place where both could hold: six kinds of member, each with a reason to come.

We started with the founders' own words.

“If East West Club didn't exist, what would be missing from your life?”the question on the workshop board

I would not have a friend circle that is specifically from the EWC.

going to a game, to a museum to have dinner with friends

maybe EWC can serve as a way of breaking monotony

the founders' answers on the board, as they wrote them

Then we asked who else that could be true for. The competition wasn't another club. It was what they'd do otherwise.

Six members, each written down. Two of their pages:

The Quiet ConnectorThe Country-Club GuyThe Semi-Retired OperatorThe New-to-Seattle FounderThe Empty-NesterThe Mid-Career High Earner
