# EWC case study: Kahran's answers to the bet (6 Nov 2026)

Source: Kahran, spoken, 6 Nov 2026. The answers are to the 21 questions in
`studies/sitemap/bet.json` (Study 72), held against the EWC output (Study 50).
No client names or exact figures here: the repo is public. Lightly cleaned
up; his phrasing is kept where it carries the idea.

## The frame

EWC's ask was to make it tangible where the club sits in a member's life, and that it isn't
formal: you don't plan a weekend for it or wait for an event. Hence a Tuesday. "Nothing ever
happens on Tuesday." It's not Monday (blowing off steam), not Wednesday (the slump), not
Thursday (already going out). What would they be doing otherwise on a Tuesday? That is the
real competition.

## By belief

**I get what this is**
- *What do they make?* The way people connect with us: how we talk to people, what matters
  about us, where we explain ourselves, where we exist.
- *Clear after ten seconds?* The words carry too much. The visuals need to carry more; that's
  what makes it clear.
- *Someone like me?* The words should do this. Show that we think about these problems
  intellectually, and keep two tones apart, as the deck did: the italic commentary voice
  versus the voice written for the company. The switch shows we can do meta-analysis, and
  that is who we want to attract.

**That's my situation**
- *A problem shaped like mine?* Name EWC's problem as breaking down the value they had, so
  the reader hears "I have that problem."
- *Understood before explaining?* Visuals. The current ones help but are very wordy.
- *Need this without failing at my job?* Show something nobody had thought of. Make the case
  for WHY a Tuesday: there's a reason it's not a random day. Insight that reads as "these
  guys are good," without making the reader feel they were supposed to have it.

**Waiting is costing me**
- *Cost of staying stuck:* show the money or effort spent on the wrong customer. For EWC: no
  money in hand meant no construction, and that held back the next customers. Knowing who
  you target and with what decides who you attract.
- *Wanting, not fear:* we made it feel accessible, so they weren't acting from fear.
- *Acts on it:* (no separate answer)

**They've done it before**
- *What did it do for them?* More revenue: 88% of their founding members signed,
  faster than they expected (heard on the call, 6 Nov).
- *Want mine to feel like that?* Yes: the problem solved, and you attract the customers you
  want.
- *Proud to sit next to it?* Leave room for pride. A quote from the client, or something else
  that shows the client is proud of it.

**They'd see what I can't**
- Answered by the writing, the verbiage and the imagery we choose. No separate notes.

**I want those two in the room**
- *Who would I work with?* We don't say. A line like "a two-person team ran the interviews."

**I know what happens if I write**
- *Ask / how long / cost:* a small, consistently formatted tag on every case study (maybe
  the bottom right): "EWC · strategy · 2 weeks · $5–10K" (a range, so we don't commit to a
  price).
- *Safe, not a pitch:* give a way to show interest without asking for contact: download
  something, comment, leave an email.
- *Without having it worked out:* (open) maybe the case study asks the reader a question,
  resolves it, then asks whether they'd have solved it that way and invites their answer.
  The case study as a conversation.

## Ideas to study (one each)

1. Why a Tuesday: the insight made visible, with visuals doing the work the words do now.
2. Two voices: commentary italics vs the client's voice, as in the deck.
3. The outcome and the pride: 88% of founding members, plus a client quote.
4. The project tag: team · time · price range, the same on every case study.
5. Interest without a pitch: download / comment / email, or the case study that asks back.
