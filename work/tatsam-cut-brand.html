A wellbeing brand that leads with what's strong in you.

Most mental-health brands start from what's wrong. Tatsam starts from what's right — a calm, human identity built to meet a stressed professional gently. We built the whole book: the logo and what it means, the full colour system with its reasoning, the photography and iconography rules, a voice with three hard rules (each with its own never) — and recording rules for every audio and video file the company would ever ship. This page is the book, opened.

the identity

A reflected "+" — the same mark, made gentler.

The guide covers presence, communication and philosophy, for internal and external audiences — and none of it was decreed from a studio. It came out of multiple deep brainstorming sessions and generative workshops with the founders and team. The logo carries the whole argument in one gesture. The brand book, verbatim:

"+ is a sign associated with trusted healthcare. But this goes beyond that, there is a reflection. The idea of going from one side to the other. And a softer more gentler way of showing care."

Two offerings carry the belief to users — because, in the book's words, "no singular solution can tackle all the problems associated with mental and holistic health." Their definitions, verbatim:

Self care

"We give recommendations that help on the path to self growth. A self paced path that goes beyond changing habits and focuses on changing mindset."

Coaching

"Our curated selection of coaches in every domain from mental counselling to career, physical health and nutrition are there to give the needed guidance."

the visual language

Colour, photography and iconography — each with a reason on the page.

As a mental-health company, Tatsam needed an inviting, calming and comforting visual language — colours and shapes that create a sense of camaraderie and intimacy that kickstarts the relationship even before a user speaks to a therapist. The emotional brief, verbatim from the book: "A sense of calm, contentment, freshness and natural, uncontaminated existence is at the basis of the Tatsam brand. All visuals should support this basic emotional vocabulary."

beliefs & voice

Three voice rules — each with its own never.

A brand voice is a promise about how you'll be treated. The book opens it plainly: "What one says is as important as how one says. Tatsam is focused on having a voice that aligns with our purpose and values." Then it commits — three rules, each paired with the failure mode it forbids, verbatim:

Empathetic

"We will always be non judgmental, understanding and come from a space of acceptance."Neither paternalistic nor enabling.

Empowering

"We will always ensure that we empower with our words and with our methods, to make change not only possible but achievable."Never taunting or making failure feel like a negative.

Liberating

"We will bring new knowledge forward in a way to liberate from misconceptions and percieved limitations."Agency not boundaries.

voice across mediums

The voice holds in audio and video, too.

Most brand books stop at sentences. This one writes recording rules — so the user's experience aligns whether they consume visuals, audio or video. For every audio practice Tatsam ships:

Clear voice

Recordings done in a clear manner with proper enunciation, in whichever language is applicable.No over edited or artifical sounding voice.

No noise

"Recording environment should be free of all noise."

No background music

"Regardless of the calmness of music, we should keep focus on just the voice of instructor."No use of music to fill silence.

And for every instruction video:

Bright and well lit

Always a well-lit room, "with a natural airy feeling to the environment."Embody the spirit of tatsam brand in instruction videos too.

Clear focus

"Focus of videos should always be on the instructor, showing from multiple angles if the task requires clarity."No jerky, rapid or unnecesary camera movements.

To the point

"Instruction videos should be as short as possible, and complete focus should be to provide the instructions."Short is better.

The same voice then ran through every screen and every slide — the brand wasn't a cover, it was the spine.

A voice means nothing until you can hold it in your hand.
