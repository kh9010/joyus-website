Pratham USA · Case study — research & strategy → story

A donation is a transaction. How do you turn it into a relationship?

Pratham USA raises funds in America for Pratham, one of India's largest education non-profits. Donors gave — but the relationship often stayed shallow, tied to whoever first brought them in, and Pratham's story wasn't reaching them in a way that mattered. We ran donor interviews, staff interviews and a donor survey to understand how people actually move from a first gift to a lifelong bond — and where that journey was leaking.

the brief, examined

Donors weren't the problem. The journey was un-designed.

Pratham USA didn't lack generous people. It lacked a designed path for them — so donors gave once and drifted, relationships stayed dependent on the person who introduced them, and the organisation couldn't see what made a donor go deep. Before recommending anything, we listened:

Donor interviews PUSA staff interviews A donor survey

The brief we handed back was deliberately strategic: stop measuring donors by "amount donated in the last 12 months," and start understanding the emotional needs that move someone from curious to committed.

↓ so we mapped the whole arc — curious stranger to lifelong patron
the donor journey

Six stages, in two halves.

The journey has two distinct parts that need different tactics. In Phase 1, Pratham is still outside the donor, trying to find a space in their life. In Phase 2, the donor has internalised Pratham — and now needs ways to express that. Each stage runs on a different emotional need.

Phase 1 · finding a space

From stranger to "I believe in this"

  • Unaware — curiosity, optimism, surprise
  • Testing the waters — trust, interest, excitement
  • Casual connection — belief, confidence, motivation
Phase 2 · becoming part of them

From "I believe" to "this is my legacy"

  • Connected — motivation, agency, inspiration
  • Deepened connection — hope, connection, meaning
  • Permanent bond — self-actualisation, legacy, identity

↓ then we asked — which touchpoints actually move people?
the touchpoint audit

Email does everything — and that's the problem.

We scored every touchpoint on how often donors engage, how much impact it has, and the effort it costs Pratham. Email carries nearly all communication. But the touchpoints that actually deepen a donor — 1:1 conversations, long-form publications, and site visits — were the most underused.

One survey finding pointed the way: people who value Pratham's long-form publications tend to be higher-value donors.

↓ and behind the touchpoints — the story itself was leaking
where the story leaks

Great stories start in India and lose energy on the way to the donor.

We mapped how information travels — from Pratham India and the chapters, through the marketing team, out to the donor — and found it leaking at every level: unclear roles, generic emails, unsupported chapter leaders, and the best storytelling from the field never making the trip.

↓ which gave us three problems worth solving next
the diagnosis

Three problems, each a place to build.

Problem 01

Donor journeys were never built with intention.

Problem 02

Pratham's processes are reactive, not leveraged.

Problem 03

One number — donations in 12 months — can't measure real engagement.

For each, we mapped directions and concrete next actions — from a "welcome to Pratham" email series and digital site visits, to a system that finally segments donors by depth, not just dollars.

what we left them with

A map of the donor — and where to invest to deepen the bond.

A shared journey model. Six stages and the emotional need behind each — language the whole team can use to talk about donors.

An honest touchpoint audit. Proof that email is overworked and the high-impact moments are underused.

The leaks, named. Exactly where Pratham's story loses energy between India and the donor.

Three problems, scoped. Each with directions and next actions for a focused build phase.
