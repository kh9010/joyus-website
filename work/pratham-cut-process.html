a process cut — pratham

How we got there. Six weeks of work, three problems, seven touchpoints, six directions.

The main page shows the answer — the rings, the shift from cause to a roomful of people doing the work. This page shows how we got to that answer: who we talked to, what we found, and what we told Pratham to do about it.

i. how the engagement ran

Six weeks, in this order.

Not a brand sprint. A structured listening exercise — we spent the first three weeks understanding what Pratham’s donors actually wanted, how Pratham was positioned in their charitable ecosystem, and where in their journey people moved or stalled. Weeks four through six turned what we’d learned into a journey map, a set of diagnostics, and a slate of recommendations.

Week 1

Org mapping

Identified the key owners of ideas inside Pratham — chapter leaders, dev staff, leadership — and spoke to them about how each one defined the problem Pratham solves.

Week 2

Emotional needs & donor conversations

Drafted a hypothesis of what was driving activation. Then started donor conversations — chapter organizers, retained majors, lapsed donors — to test whether the hypothesis held against actual experience.

Week 3

Positioning

Mapped where Pratham sat in each donor’s broader charitable ecosystem. Who was Pratham “in competition” with for share-of-mind — and on what basis?

Week 4

Journey mapping

Customer-journey mapping for the donor: entry points, exit points, the moments where commitment deepened. The two ring diagrams (the main page’s spine) came out of this week.

Week 5

Recommendations

Synthesised the journey work into a slate of recommendations for the Pratham team and a scoped Phase 2 to act on them.

Week 6+

Shared understanding

Walked leadership through what we’d found — including a clear-eyed read of the share of donation capital Pratham was winning vs. competing nonprofits, and what would be needed to grow it.

ii. what we found wrong

Three structural problems, naming the same root.

The donor-side findings traced back to three things on the org side. They’re different angles on one underlying issue: Pratham was operating on a one-dimensional read of what donor commitment actually was.

i.

Pratham was reactive in its processes.

Donor communication was triggered by external events — a gala, a tax-receipt deadline, an annual report drop — rather than by where the donor was in their own journey with Pratham. The org responded; it didn’t shape.

ii.

Donor journeys hadn’t been constructed with intentionality.

There was no shared internal model of what a donor’s arc through Pratham was supposed to feel like. Chapters improvised in good faith and produced wildly different experiences of the same org from one city to the next.

iii.

The metric was one-dimensional.

“Donation in the last 12 months” was the dashboard’s headline. That number flattened relational depth into a dollar bucket — and quietly mis-categorised some of Pratham’s most committed donors as mid-tier because their current-year gift wasn’t the biggest in the room.

iv. what we told pratham to do

Six directions out of the diagnosis.

Half of these were systems work (the way the org runs day-to-day). The other half were experience work (the way donors meet the org). Together they answered the three problems above without trying to replace any of Pratham’s existing strengths.

i.

Build a system to simplify and speed up digital asset creation.

Chapter leaders were spending hours re-making the same images, decks, and one-pagers. A small shared asset system would free up that time for donor work.

ii.

Find and build automations for existing processes.

Post-event follow-up, tax-receipt cadence, and the lapsed-donor outreach loop were all being run by hand. Automating the rote bits would let the team spend their attention where it actually moved the journey.

iii.

Expand systems for donors to volunteer non-monetary assets.

Several Connected and Deepened donors wanted to give time, skill, or network access — not just money — and Pratham had no formal channel for it. Building one would broaden how donors could deepen.

iv.

Build ways for passionate donors to engage more deeply.

Includes the “Humans of Pratham” series — donor-led storytelling, in donors’ own words, surfaced to other donors at earlier stages of their journey. The deepest donors were a content asset Pratham wasn’t using.

v.

Identify & strengthen the experiences that already moved people in.

The site visit was the strongest single thing Pratham had — Strong impact, but Rarely-frequency. The recommendation was to build out a digital site-visit program (the DLV program) that gave a larger set of donors access to the same seminal experience, without the trip cost. Same moment, more vehicles.

vi.

Create measures beyond “donation in the last 12 months.”

Add depth signals — site-visit attendance, multi-year retention, publication engagement, inter-chapter referrals, family-level involvement — and report them alongside dollars. Sort by relationship, not by amount.
