We listened to donors before we told Pratham anything.

The recommendations only matter if the picture underneath is true. This is that picture — the six-stage donor journey, the touchpoint evaluation, and what donors actually take away from the moments that move them most.

the donor journey

Six stages, each running on a different emotional need.

A donor doesn't go from stranger to patron in one leap. They pass through stages — and what they need to move forward changes at each one. We split it into two phases: Pratham finding a space in their life, then the donor making Pratham part of theirs.

Phase 1 · Pratham is still outside them

01

Unaware

Has never heard of Pratham. "This seems interesting."

curiosity · optimism · surprise
02

Testing the waters

Heard of it casually, maybe via someone they know. "This seems legitimate."

trust · interest · excitement
03

Casual connection

Attended an event or gave once. "I believe in this."

belief · confidence · motivation

Phase 2 · the donor has internalised Pratham

04

Connected

Has given more than once; finds the work personally meaningful. "I want this to succeed."

motivation · agency · inspiration
05

Deepened connection

Ready to be involved beyond the donation. "This is part of my life."

hope · connection · meaning
06

Permanent bond

Years in, gives time and energy. "This is my legacy."

self-actualisation · legacy · identity

For each stage we also mapped the touchpoints, artifacts, narratives and owners — and the kind of donor who lives there, from "a typical Microsoft or Google employee" to a decade-long patron.

the touchpoint audit

We scored every way Pratham talks to a donor.

Seven touchpoints, six questions — three quantitative (how often, how much impact, how much effort) and three qualitative (why the donor engages, which Pratham goal it serves, which donor goal it meets).

the three underused moments

What donors actually take away.

We went deep on the three high-impact touchpoints Pratham wasn't using enough — because what a donor walks away feeling is the real product.

Long-form publications

  • Feel more connected to India
  • Like the simplicity of "pay it forward for education"
  • Being part of a team that's winning
  • Social status; finding like-minded people

1:1 conversations

  • Get talking points about Pratham & education
  • Give because their social circle does
  • The positive feeling of altruism
  • Feel they're alleviating an injustice

Site visits

  • A tangible sense of impact
  • A sense of purpose
  • Memories and stories they relate to for years
  • Talking points to share onward

And the survey confirmed it: people who value Pratham's long-form publications tend to be the higher-value donors.

This is who the donor is. Now — how does Pratham reach them?
