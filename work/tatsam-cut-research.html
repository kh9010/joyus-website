The case for Tatsam, in the data.

Before a brand or a product, the question was simple: is this real? Our wellbeing report answered it — measuring how modern professionals are actually doing, and finding the gap between how fine they look and how fine they feel.

the headline finding

The crisis goes all the way to the top.

The people you'd assume are thriving — senior executives — were, in large numbers, scoring at the bottom of the wellbeing scale.

59.2%

of male executives scored below low or very low in overall wellbeing.

58.4%

of female executives scored below low or very low, too — the gap isn't about gender.

Hidden

The struggle is largely invisible — which is exactly why a workplace product has to go looking for it.

If the people at the top are this unwell, "manage your stress" was never going to be the answer.

the five domains · PERMA

Below average in every domain that matters.

The PERMA Profiler breaks wellbeing into five measurable domains. The share of people scoring below average, domain by domain:

52.4%

Positive emotions — contentment & joy

66.7%

Engagement — absorption in work & life

59.4%

Relationships — satisfaction overall

62.2%

Meaning in life — purpose & direction

45.0%

Accomplishment — the strongest of a weak field

Two patterns under the numbers: health, flow and positive emotions connected significantly to presenteeism and absenteeism — and the lower engagement and accomplishment scores clustered in people with over five years of professional experience. The longer you'd been working, the worse it got.

the social & organisational picture

Lonely at work is a line item.

The social wellbeing and organisational happiness indices put texture on the headline:

30%

reported flatly that they felt lonely.

47%

scored below average when asked about feeling loved and supported.

1 in 3

reported low social support — and low respect from colleagues.

And the cost shows up in the org's behaviour, not just its mood. Low happiness alone accounted for a 33.2% increase in presenteeism, a 66.7% decrease in engagement and positive organisational behaviour, and a 49.8% decrease across relationships, physical health and employee resilience. High stress, anxiety and low mood emerged as major drivers of 76% work-performance impairment and 56% presenteeism.

the upside · psychological capital

The same dataset held the business case.

Psychological capital — hope, efficacy, resilience, optimism — measured at 32.2%, with a modelled headroom of +40%. Played through, that one gain reads as +63% work happiness & satisfaction, +22% performance, and +36% positive organisational citizenship — with optimism alone carrying a +44% swing.

There were bright spots to build on, not just deficits to fix: 40.6% scored themselves above average in positive emotions, 37.8% above average in meaningfulness. That's why Tatsam grows strengths instead of cataloguing what's wrong — the upside was the largest number in the report.

Diagnosis said: it's bad everywhere. The model said: the fastest fix is growing what's already strong.

the report

Wellbeing, measured — not assumed.

We turned the wellbeing data into a report leadership could actually read — clear visuals, honest numbers, and the through-line that became Tatsam's reason to exist.

A real need earns a real identity.
